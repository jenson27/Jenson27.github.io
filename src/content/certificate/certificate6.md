This e-module from the Centre for Communication Skills at Singapore Institute of Technology builds the skills needed to write effective critical reflections, a key component of academic and professional development. It moves beyond simple description of events to show how reflective writing can be structured to demonstrate genuine learning and insight.

The module walks through established models of reflection, including Gibbs' Reflective Cycle and Borton's "What? So What? Now What?" framework, and uses annotated real-world examples to show how a well-organised reflection integrates description, analysis, and theory to arrive at a meaningful conclusion. It also covers the tone, vocabulary, and first-person voice appropriate for reflective academic writing, as distinct from more formal, objective academic prose.

<ul>
  <li>Distinguish between mere description of an event and genuine critical reflection that explores and analyses its significance.</li>
  <li>Apply structured models of reflection, such as Gibbs' Reflective Cycle and Borton's three-stage model, to organise personal or professional experiences.</li>
  <li>Structure a reflective piece of writing into clear stages: description, exploration and analysis, and outcome or conclusion.</li>
  <li>Integrate relevant theory or literature to deepen the analysis of an experience, rather than relying on description alone.</li>
  <li>Use appropriate reflective vocabulary and a calm, honest, first-person tone to convey feelings and thoughts without sounding overly emotional or unsupported.</li>
  <li>Draw forward-looking conclusions that identify specific skills, knowledge, or actions to develop as a result of the reflective process.</li>
</ul>

By the end of the module, learners are able to produce well-organised, analytical reflective writing suitable for academic assignments and professional practice, and are awarded a certificate of completion from the Centre for Communication Skills, Singapore Institute of Technology.
